# 🎬 MOVIE ADAPTATION PACK — *The Whistle of the Iroko*

*A cinematic guide for adapting the story to film — pairing with the audiobook voice reels.*

---

## 1. LOGLINE

> A Lagos journalist returns to her grandmother's village in Enugu to bury a woman who "died in her sleep" — only to discover that her grandmother was the secret keeper of a grove, a diary, and the names of fourteen trafficked girls, and that the men who killed her are coming to the funeral.

**Genre:** Suspense drama / family mystery / social thriller.
**Tone:** Lagos energy in the city; slow-burn dread in the village; one opera of a funeral in Act III.
**Runtime target:** ~1h 50m (feature) or a 3-part limited series.

---

## 3. ACT STRUCTURE (chapter → screen beat)

**ACT I — "The Call" (Ch. 1–3)**
- Cold open: 3:47 a.m. phone call; we never see the grandmother's face. The line dies.
- Morning: "Your grandmother is dead." The funeral must be rushed. A coffin nobody will open.
- Journey: night bus, Ore roadblock, the scarred man who doesn't look at her, the girl pressed against the window glass.
- Arrival: the compound in mourning, the coffin in the parlor, the whisper: *witch*.

**ACT II — "The Grove" (Ch. 4–8)**
- Midnight: she opens the coffin. Blue lips. Bare feet. A whistle appears on the parlor floor.
- The diary inside the Bible's hollow cover. The deed from 1911. The list of fourteen names.
- The night dig under the third root. The key inside the whistle. The box: photographs, a senator, a letter.
- The fire. The raid on her room. The scarred man — Inspector Adebayo, her grandmother's informant.
- The mission house at night: five girls on mattresses, the woman in lace moving among them.
- Veranda confession: Emeka names Ifeoma. The deal he signed. *"If you talk, your son will die."*

**ACT III — "The Funeral" (Ch. 9–10 + Epilogue)**
- The convoy. Odogwu's condolence visit — the audience knows what the mourners don't.
- Graveside: "Wait." The custom of the last look. The photograph sewn into the hem.
- The whistle: three long blasts. The guardians of the grove rise from the bush.
- The chase to the iroko. The gunshot. Emeka takes the bullet meant for Chidi.
- The girls walk out of the mission house into daylight.
- Eulogy: *"By that definition, my grandmother was the most powerful witch this village has ever produced."* The volley of Dane guns.
- Final scene: dawn at the iroko. She blows the whistle once. The mist moves. She walks away.

## 4. SAMPLE SCREENPLAY — OPENING SCENE

```
FADE IN:

INT. CHIDI'S FLAT, SURULERE, LAGOS — 3:47 A.M.

Dark. One desk lamp. A laptop glow on a young woman's face —
CHIDINMA OKAFOR (26), ink on her fingers, a mug gone cold.

Her phone BUZZES. Screen: MAMA NNENNA.

She picks up. No hello.

                    MAMA NNENNA (V.O.)
        (urgent, low)
        Chidi. Ha na-egwu ala n'okpuru osisi iroko.

                    CHIDI
        (rubbing her eyes)
        Grandma. Kedu. It is 3:47 in the morning—

                    MAMA NNENNA (V.O.)
        They are digging under the iroko tree.
        Three nights now. I have heard them.

EXT. ODA VILLAGE — NIGHT (INSERT)

An IROKO TREE, vast as a cathedral. Two torches move at its
roots. Earth being thrown up. The diggers work WITHOUT LIGHT,
like men who do not want to be seen.

BACK TO SCENE

                    MAMA NNENNA (V.O.)
        If I die before you come home — do not let
        them put me in the ground. My body is not
        the only thing that would go into that ground.

The line CRACKLES. Goes dead.

Chidi stares at the phone. Outside, Lagos hums, indifferent.

                    CHIDI (V.O.)
        My grandmother called me at 3:47 in the
        morning. She did not say hello. She said:
        they are digging under the iroko tree.
        The next morning, she was dead.

HARD CUT TO:

INT. OJOTA MOTORPARK, LAGOS — DUSK
```

## 5. SOUND DESIGN & MUSIC NOTES

- **Leitmotif — The Whistle:** three long blasts recorded on a real brass referee whistle, pitched and reverb'd. It opens the film (black screen), returns at the coffin, at the graveside, and closes the film. The audience should flinch when they hear it.
- **Music:** talking drum under the night-bus sequence; a lone *ogene* (metal gong) for the grove; church choir swelling at the funeral that slowly detunes as dread arrives. Score restraint in Act II — the diary pages should be read in near-silence.
- **Textures:** 3:47 a.m. Lagos (distant okada, frogs, generator hum); Enugu motorpark at dawn (akara frying, diesel); village night (crickets, wind in the iroko, one cough of a generator).
- **The scream:** the woman in lace's scream at the graveside — the moment the mask drops — should be a sound the whole film has been waiting for.
- **Silence:** after the gunshot at the iroko, hold 3 full seconds of bush silence before the guardians move. Let the audience hear what the village hears.
