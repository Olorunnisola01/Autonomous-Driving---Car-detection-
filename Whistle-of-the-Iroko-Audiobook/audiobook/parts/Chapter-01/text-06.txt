I sat in the dark of my Surulere room with the phone in my hand and the smell of the harmattan dust outside, and I told myself the old woman had been drinking too much of her medicine. Old people wake up in the middle of the night and their minds go walking in strange places. By morning she would have forgotten. By morning I would call her and she would laugh and say "Adịghị m mma n'ụra" — I was not well in my sleep — and we would both pretend. But I did not sleep. I sat at the table and watched the phone the way you watch a snake that might move. At 6:12 a.m., the sun was already ripping holes in the Lagos sky, and the call came. Not from Mama Nnenna. From Uncle Emeka. I knew it was bad before he spoke, because my uncle — who drank palm wine from noon and sang at funerals — did not sing.
