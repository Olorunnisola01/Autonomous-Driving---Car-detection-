My grandmother called me at 3:47 in the morning, and she did not say hello. She said: "Chidi, ha na-egwu ala n'okpuru osisi iroko." They are digging under the iroko tree. I was awake anyway, which is the only reason I answered. I was hunched over my laptop at the small wooden table in the one-room flat I rented in Surulere, Lagos — a room so small I could touch the fridge and the wardrobe at the same time if I stretched.
