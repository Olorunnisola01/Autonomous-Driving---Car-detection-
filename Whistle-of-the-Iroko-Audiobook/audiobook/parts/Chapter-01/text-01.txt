My grandmother called me at 3:47 in the morning, and she did not say hello. She said: "Chidi, ha na-egwu ala n'okpuru osisi iroko." They are digging under the iroko tree. I was awake anyway, which is the only reason I answered. I was hunched over my laptop at the small wooden table in the one-room flat I rented in Surulere, Lagos — a room so small I could touch the fridge and the wardrobe at the same time if I stretched. I had been working on the story that had eaten six months of my life: fourteen missing girls. Fourteen, all from the East, all between fifteen and nineteen years old, all vanished within eighteen months, and on every single police file someone had written the same lazy sentence in the same lazy handwriting: Ran away from home. I was twenty-six years old, a journalist with the National Mirror, and that night I had finally found something.
