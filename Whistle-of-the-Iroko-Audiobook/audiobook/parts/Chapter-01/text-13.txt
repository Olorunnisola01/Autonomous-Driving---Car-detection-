At 6:12 a.m., the sun was already ripping holes in the Lagos sky, and the call came. Not from Mama Nnenna. From Uncle Emeka. I knew it was bad before he spoke, because my uncle — who drank palm wine from noon and sang at funerals — did not sing. He cleared his throat four times and then he said, in the flat voice of a man reading a telegram: "Chidinma, nne gi anwụọla." Your grandmother is dead.
