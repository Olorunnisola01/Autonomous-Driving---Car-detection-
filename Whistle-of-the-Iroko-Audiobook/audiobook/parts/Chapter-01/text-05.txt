Three long blasts, and the sound carried over the cassava fields, over the stream, and every child in the compound would scatter home like chickens before rain. The whistle that brings the moon, the village children called it. "Keep it close," Mama Nnenna said. "The whistle is the key. And Chidi —" She took a breath. It was a small sound, but it was the sound of someone holding back a much larger sound. "I loved you from the day your mother put you in my arms and went back to Lagos to work. You were a difficult baby. You cried for no reason. But I loved you." "Grandma, why are you talking like this? Should I come home? Should I come now?" The line crackled. Somewhere between Enugu and Lagos, a tower swallowed her voice and gave back only noise. I said her name three times, and the noise became a dial tone.
