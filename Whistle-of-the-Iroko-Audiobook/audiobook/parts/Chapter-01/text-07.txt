He cleared his throat four times and then he said, in the flat voice of a man reading a telegram: "Chidinma, nne gi anwụọla." Your grandmother is dead. "She died in her sleep," he said, before I could ask. "Monday night. They found her this morning. The body is already swollen. We have to bury her quickly, Chidi. The body cannot spend many nights. It is the custom." I heard the words. I heard them the way you hear thunder from far away — you know it is coming, but you cannot yet feel the ground shake. And then, very quietly, at the back of my mind, something my grandmother had said began to ring. Not like a phone. Like a whistle. ---
