She wears my friend's wrapper and she smiles with my friend's smile, but she is not my friend." "Grandma, you are scaring me now—" "Good. Fear is a lamp. Walk by it." Then her voice dropped, and what she said next I have repeated to myself a thousand times since, trying to find the meaning I missed: "If I die before you come home, do not let them put me in the ground. Whatever they tell you — that I was sick, that I died in my sleep, that the body is bad, that the funeral must be rushed — do not let them bury me. My body is not the only thing that would go into that ground, Chidi." "Grandma—" "You remember the whistle? The old brass one? The one I used to blow when I called you in from the bush at dusk?" "Yes," I whispered. I remembered it the way you remember a smell — instantly, whole.
